## 5 Summary

This literature review examines recent research related to generative business canvas systems from four closely connected perspectives: datasets and evaluation metrics, business decision support systems, large language model reasoning, and multi-agent, knowledge-enhanced, and visually structured analytical systems.

The review first shows that current benchmark construction has made clear progress in evaluating business and finance reasoning, financial question answering, and business intelligence insight generation. Benchmarks such as BizBench, FinanceBench, FinanceReasoning, and BI-Bench demonstrate that business analysis tasks require not only text understanding, but also numerical inference, evidence grounding, and insight-oriented evaluation. At the same time, no existing benchmark directly evaluates a generative business canvas system as a transparent and collaborative analytical workspace.

Second, the literature on business decision support and explainable AI indicates that modern analytical systems are no longer judged only by predictive performance. Trust, transparency, interpretability, and human usability have become essential requirements. However, current DSS and BI systems still largely emphasize output presentation rather than reasoning process externalization.

Third, the review finds that large language models have significantly expanded the capabilities of intelligent systems in planning, summarization, and reasoning, while multi-agent research has further strengthened task decomposition, collaboration, feedback, and tool use. At the same time, RAG, agentic RAG, knowledge graph enhancement, and graph-based reasoning provide strong foundations for evidence-grounded and structurally organized inference. Yet these advances are still mostly studied in isolation.

Finally, the visual analytics and explainable interface literature suggests that the future of complex AI-supported analysis is unlikely to remain confined to linear dialogue or dashboard-style presentation. Instead, there is increasing evidence that users benefit from structured, inspectable, and editable analytical workspaces. This directly supports the motivation for a business canvas system in which business elements, evidence, reasoning paths, and strategic conclusions can be represented and revised visually.

Taken together, the reviewed literature supports a clear conclusion: current research has already produced the main technical foundations required for a generative business canvas system, but their systematic integration remains insufficient. Therefore, the design and implementation of a generative business canvas system based on multi-agent collaborative reasoning is both theoretically meaningful and practically valuable.
